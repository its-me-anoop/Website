# UK GP Practice Website Suppliers — Market Comparison (2025/2026)

## TL;DR

The market is large and fragmented (NHS England's own audit found **55 distinct website suppliers** across the practices it sampled, with **9 main suppliers covering ~70%+** of that sample — [SCW case study, Feb 2026](https://scwcsu.nhs.uk/case-studies/navigating-the-digital-front-door-improving-gp-websites-for-a-mobile-device)). Four suppliers matter most for a competitive read: **My Surgery Website (MSW)**, the incumbent volume leader; **Footfall/Foundation (Silicon Practice)**, the "digital front door" suite that bundles website + triage + clinical-system integration most aggressively; **iatro Practice365** (now owned by Agilio Software), MSW's closest direct rival on features; and **Tree View Designs**, the best-reviewed boutique specialist. A long tail of smaller independents (GenPra, GPsurgery.net, GP Surgery Sites/Neighbourhood Direct, SurgeryWeb, Websites4GPs, N3i, Engage Health Systems) compete mainly on price and personal support rather than platform sophistication. **EMIS/Optum, TPP and Cegedim/INPS are not public-website suppliers at all** — they sell patient-portal *apps* (Patient Access, SystmOnline, Vision Online) that sit behind a practice website; conflating them with the website suppliers above is a category error worth flagging back to whoever compiled the original brief. "Numed iScribe" also isn't a website product — Numed Healthcare is a diagnostics-equipment/interoperability vendor.

The single most decision-relevant finding for your fictional Willowbrook Surgery demo: **NHS England's own service manual explicitly tells practices *not* to use accessibility/translation overlay widgets** (ReciteMe, Browsealoud/ReachDeck) — despite these being commonly bolted onto NHS trust sites — because they "cannot make a website fully compliant... and cannot protect you from legal action," obscure content, and conflict with assistive tech ([service-manual.nhs.uk/accessibility/development](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/accessibility/development)). Willowbrook currently has no such widget, which is *correct* per NHS guidance, not a gap — worth stating explicitly in the accessibility statement rather than leaving it to look like an oversight.

## 1. The market structure and how vendors are evaluated

NHS England runs a free **GP Website Benchmarking and Improvement Tool** — an Excel audit of **50 evidence-based criteria**, built from usability research with 102 participants of low-to-moderate digital/English confidence observed on 10 real GP sites ([NHS England publication](https://www.england.nhs.uk/publication/gp-website-benchmarking-and-improvement-tool/); [Management in Practice coverage](https://managementinpractice.com/news/gp-website-improvement-and-benchmarking-tool-launched-by-nhs-england/)). It explicitly separates **supplier responsibility** (templates, nav structure, mobile responsiveness, platform accessibility, form functionality, technical compliance) from **practice responsibility** (content accuracy, reading level, keeping pages current, third-party link health). This is the industry's de facto scorecard — several vendors (Silicon Practice, GPsurgery.net's "Navigator" template) market themselves explicitly against the score it produces.

NHS England's companion **"Step-by-step guide to improving general practice website online journeys"** ([england.nhs.uk](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/step-by-step-guide-to-improving-general-practice-website-online-journeys/)) defines the canonical information architecture almost every vendor below converges on:

- **Four priority pages**: homepage, appointments, prescriptions, contact.
- **Core patient tasks driving structure**: book/change/cancel appointment, request a repeat prescription, view test results, request a sick/fit note, register with the practice, find contact/opening-hours info.
- Content should target a **9–11 reading age**, use NHS England's provided content templates rather than bespoke copy, and link out to nhs.uk rather than duplicating clinical content locally.

**This maps almost exactly onto Willowbrook's existing IA** (primary: book/cancel appointment + repeat prescription; secondary: symptoms online, register, test results, sick/fit note) — that's a genuine point of credibility for the demo site to lean on, e.g. in sales copy ("built to the same task hierarchy NHS England's own research prescribes"), rather than a generic claim.

## 3. Not website suppliers (common conflation to flag)

The original brief's list included several names that are **not** GP-website CMS vendors and shouldn't be benchmarked against the list above:

- **EMIS Web / Optum** — the dominant GP clinical system (~55% of English practices, ~39–40M patient records). It does not sell a public marketing website; the patient-facing product is **Patient Access**, a separate app/portal for booking, prescriptions, records and test results ([emishealth.com/patient-access](https://www.emishealth.com/patient-access)). A practice on EMIS still needs one of the CMS vendors above for its public site, which then deep-links into Patient Access.
- **TPP (SystmOne)** — same pattern: **SystmOnline** is the patient portal, not a public website product ([tpp-uk.com](https://tpp-uk.com/general-practice/)).
- **Cegedim / INPS (Vision)** — **Vision Online Services** was the equivalent patient portal, but **In Practice Systems Ltd was placed into administration on 10 December 2024** — a genuine deal-breaker/vendor-risk data point if this ever comes up in the demo's competitive narrative, since it shows the clinical-systems-adjacent tier of this market isn't immune to failure.
- **Numed Healthcare** — a Sheffield diagnostics-equipment and clinical-system-integration vendor (I3 software linking EMIS/SystmOne/Vision to diagnostic devices). No product called "iScribe" tied to GP websites was found; the closest name match (iscribehealth.com) is a US ambient AI-scribe company unrelated to UK GP websites or Numed. Treat "Numed iScribe" as a mis-mapped name in the original brief.
- **Livi** — a private, video-first GP consultation provider (part of Kry International), not a practice-website CMS vendor, despite appearing in Practice Index's website-supplier directory.
- **Accurx, Patchs Health, Klinik, Anima** — these are **online-consultation/triage engines**, the layer that sits *behind* a "tell us about your symptoms online" link on a practice website (exactly Willowbrook's secondary task). They are not website CMSs. Silicon Practice's own comparison document profiles them alongside its own FootFall product for this reason ([Silicon Practice "Top 5 UK GP Practice Solution Providers in 2024" PDF](https://siliconpractice.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Top-5-UK-GP-Practice-Solution-Providers-in-2024.pdf)) — useful primary-source detail on how triage vendors position themselves (Klinik claims up to 70% phone-traffic reduction; Patchs uses "Assign/Face-to-face/Topic/Signpost AI" to route requests; Accurx's Reception Flow lets staff submit a shortened request on a patient's behalf).

## 4. Cross-cutting feature analysis

### CMS / editing model — three real patterns, not many
1. **Purpose-built live/WYSIWYG editors** (Footfall/Foundation, iatro Practice365, GP Surgery Sites, MSW, GenPra) — edit-in-place on the rendered page, no code, role-based multi-editor permissions. This is now table stakes; every serious vendor has converged on it.
2. **WordPress, simplified** (SurgeryWeb) — leverages a familiar ecosystem but inherits WordPress's plugin/patch-management overhead, which the vendor absorbs on the customer's behalf.
3. **Managed/hybrid** (GPsurgery.net's Flexi tier, GenPra's included-hours model, Websites4GPs' "we update it for you") — practice submits change requests, vendor implements. Common for smaller/less digitally-confident practices, at the cost of same-day-or-hours turnaround rather than instant self-service.

None of the vendors surveyed publish evidence of a true **structured/headless CMS** (content modelled as data, editable via API) — everything is page-template editing. That's consistent with the sector's small teams and non-technical editors, but it's a gap if a studio wanted to differentiate the Willowbrook pitch with, e.g., a component-driven editing experience.

### Triage integration
Real depth here is bimodal: **Footfall/Foundation** is the only supplier with genuinely deep, native clinical-system integration (EMIS/SystmOne write-back, PDS verification, capacity-aware routing). Everyone else — MSW, iatro, GenPra, GPsurgery.net, Tree View, SurgeryWeb — treats triage as an **outbound link** to a third-party consultation platform (eConsult, Accurx, Klinik, Patchs, Anima) rather than owning the workflow. For a demo/sales artifact this matters: "symptoms online" as a link-out to a named or generic third-party tool is realistic and typical; building a fully native triage engine into the demo would overstate what most real GP websites actually do.

### Translation
Notably **absent as a named feature** from every vendor's marketing except Engage Health Systems (multilingual check-in kiosks, not website content) and the generic NHS-trust-wide ReciteMe/Browsealoud deployments, which are a different market (acute trusts, not GP practices) and are the pattern **NHS England's own service manual advises against** (see below). No GP-website-specific vendor markets in-house multi-language content publishing (e.g., parallel Welsh/English pages, which *would* be a real requirement for FootFall NHS Wales practices — [siliconpractice.co.uk/footfall-wales](https://siliconpractice.co.uk/footfall-wales/)). If Willowbrook wants a translation story, the realistic and NHS-guidance-compliant approach is native page content in the needed language(s) plus reliance on the visitor's own browser/OS translation tools — not a bolted-on widget.

### Accessibility toolbar — the most important finding
ReciteMe and Browsealoud/ReachDeck are widely deployed on **NHS acute/community trust** sites ([Birmingham Community NHS](https://www.bhamcommunity.nhs.uk/recite-me), [Aneurin Bevan UHB](https://abuhb.nhs.wales/news/news/using-the-reciteme-accessibility-tool-on-our-website/), [NHS England 2016 rollout announcement](https://www.england.nhs.uk/2016/05/website-accessibility/)) but **none of the GP-website CMS vendors surveyed advertise one as a feature**, and the current NHS digital service manual is explicit that overlay widgets should be avoided:

> "Accessibility widgets cannot make a website fully compliant with accessibility standards and they cannot protect you from legal action." ... "Widgets typically sit in the banner or a corner of the page, sometimes obscuring key functionality... When a user clicks on the widget, by accident or on purpose, an overlay may pop up and cover up information or create a barrier that users cannot get past." — [NHS digital service manual, Accessibility: Development](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/accessibility/development)

This is a **live, unresolved debate** in the wider NHS estate (2016 rollout vs. current guidance reversing course — see the [nhsuk-service-manual community backlog issue tracking this](https://github.com/nhsuk/nhsuk-service-manual-community-backlog/issues/449)), and it's directly relevant to Willowbrook's accessibility statement: the absence of a toolbar is defensible best practice, not a missing feature, and the statement should say so rather than reading as incomplete.

## 5. What this means for the Willowbrook demo site

1. **IA validation, not overhaul.** Willowbrook's home-page task hierarchy (book/cancel appointment + repeat prescription as primary; symptoms/register/results/sick note as secondary) already matches NHS England's four-priority-page and core-task model almost exactly. This is a legitimate, citable design decision for sales copy — "modelled on NHS England's own patient-research-led benchmarking criteria," not generic best practice hand-waving.
2. **Accessibility statement language.** Explicitly note that no overlay/toolbar is used, and why, citing the NHS service manual's own position. This differentiates Willowbrook from the (dated, trust-level) ReciteMe pattern many real NHS sites still carry, and turns a potential "why is there no accessibility button" question into a demonstrated point of expertise.
3. **Triage/"tell us about symptoms online."** Realistic framing is a hand-off to a named or generic third-party consultation tool (eConsult/Accurx/Klinik-style), not a fully native triage engine — matches what all but one real competitor (Footfall) actually does, and is far cheaper to credibly demo.
4. **Editing-experience story, if the studio wants a differentiator.** Every incumbent has converged on page-template WYSIWYG editing (in-place, drag-and-drop, role-based). None demonstrate a structured/component-based editing model. If part of the sales pitch is "better CMS than what you have now," a component-driven editing story (vs. free-form page editing) is a genuinely open differentiation angle in this specific market, not a crowded one.
5. **Category-error correction for whoever briefed this research.** EMIS/Optum, TPP, and Cegedim/Vision are clinical-system/patient-portal vendors, not website-CMS competitors; Numed doesn't make GP websites; Livi is a private video-GP service. Comparing Willowbrook against them as "website suppliers" would be comparing against the wrong products.

## Notes on confidence / what would resolve remaining uncertainty

- **MSW's exact market share and current owner** are not independently confirmable from public primary sources — the "48%" figure is a single trade-press claim of unclear date, and the FPM Group/Stericycle ownership chain could not be fully verified beyond MSW's own help-centre categorisation. Companies House filings for "GP Surgery Websites Ltd" (company no. 11092896) and for FPM Group would resolve this definitively if it matters for the pitch.
- **Pricing** is publicly transparent only for the smaller independents (SurgeryWeb, Websites4GPs, GenPra); the three largest platforms (MSW, Footfall/Foundation, iatro Practice365) use an enterprise/quote-based sales process, so no like-for-like cost comparison is possible from public sources alone — would need a direct quote request to each.
- I did not find a public copy of NHS England's **"GP website template comparison"** research document that names suppliers by score (referenced by search snippets but not located as a directly fetchable primary source) — if this exists and is obtainable via an NHS England FOI or regional ICB contact, it would be the single best source for a rigorous head-to-head score comparison rather than the vendor self-reporting relied on above.
