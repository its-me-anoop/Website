## A. Statutory/contractual "must have" content (from PRN00651 Part 4, cross-checked against GMS/PMS Regs 2015 & 2025/26 contract letters)

These derive from the GMS Regulations 2015 duty to **"maintain an online presence"** (Reg. 73) and the requirement to publish everything otherwise required in the practice leaflet (Sch. 3, Part 6) — mirrored in PMS Regulations (Reg. 66, Sch. 2 Part 6) and APMS Directions (Part 4, 14). Source: [PRN00651 PDF, pp.58–71](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/PRN00651-creating-a-highly-usable-and-accessible-gp-website-for-patients-v3.pdf), citing [GMS Regs SI 2015/1862](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/1862/contents/made) and [PMS Regs SI 2015/1879](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/1879/contents/made).

Required on the website (mirrors the practice leaflet):
- Practice name, phone number, address(es), email address — and "any other way by which a patient may contact the Contractor to book/amend an appointment or order repeat prescriptions"
- Opening hours of practice premises, and how to obtain access to services throughout core hours
- Extended hours access arrangements
- Named accountable GP arrangements (patients generally, and patients aged 75+) and patients' right to express a practitioner preference
- Practice partnership/company structure details (partner names, or company directors/secretary/shareholders + registered office)
- Full names of everyone providing services under the contract, and their professional qualifications
- Whether the practice trains healthcare professionals/students
- **Practice area / catchment boundary** — "by reference to a sketch diagram, plan, or postcode" (commonly implemented as an interactive boundary map + postcode checker)
- Disabled-access arrangements at the premises, or the alternative arrangements if none
- **How to register as a patient**
- Services available under the contract
- Criteria and method for obtaining home visits
- Out-of-hours arrangements and how patients contact them (or confirmation the ICB commissions this)
- Method for obtaining repeat prescriptions; dispensing arrangements if a dispensing practice
- **How to complain / give feedback**
- Patients' rights and responsibilities, including keeping appointments
- The zero-tolerance/violent-patient policy — action that may be taken if a patient is violent or abusive
- Who has access to patient information and patients' rights regarding disclosure
- Full contact details of the responsible ICB
- **Practices must review all published website information at least once every 12 months** (GMS Regs Part 10)

Additional statutory/near-statutory items called out separately in the same table:
- **GP net earnings** — mean net earnings of GPs at the practice for the previous financial year, published annually (GMS Regs Part 5, Reg. 21; also see [general practice pay transparency guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/general-practice-pay-transparency-guidance/))
- **Patient Participation Group (PPG)** — keep patients updated on PPG progress/agreed actions (GMS Regs Part 5)
- **Online services facility**: patients must be able to order repeat prescriptions online, view/print a list of repeatable prescription items, nominate/change a pharmacy, request and view test results, and update personal details online — underpinned by GMS Regs Part 10 "patient online services" provisions and (post-2021 amendment) PMS Reg. 64ZB–64ZD
- **An online consultation facility** is a distinct regulatory requirement (GMS Regs Part 10; PMS Reg. 64ZD; APMS Directions 14(1)(gbb)) — separate from a generic "contact us" form
- **Friends and Family Test** — collect, report and **publish results** (GMS Regs Part 14; see [FFT submission guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/publication/guidance-on-the-submission-of-gp-practice-friends-and-family-test-data/))
- **Access to Service Information (A2SI) / Directory of Services metadata** — standardised machine-readable practice data for other NHS platforms to consume

Legal-compliance items (not GP-contract-specific but cited in the same table as "must"):
- **WCAG 2.1 AA minimum** conformance (the guide's own wording — see accessibility discrepancy flagged below) + published **accessibility statement**
- Equality Act 2010 / Equality and Human Rights Commission code of practice compliance
- UK GDPR / Data Protection Act 2018 compliance, with patient-facing sections on: your medical records, privacy notices, SMS/email use, your rights, access to your record, **National Data Opt-out**
- PECR (Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations) compliance for cookies/electronic comms

Source for this whole block: [PRN00651 PDF, "Part 4: Contractual requirements of a GP website," pp.58–71](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/PRN00651-creating-a-highly-usable-and-accessible-gp-website-for-patients-v3.pdf).

## B. 2025/26 GP contract changes touching the website (effective 1 October 2025 — these are newer than, and supplement, PRN00651)

1. **Online consultation tool must stay open for the full core-hours window** — 8am–6.30pm Monday–Friday, excluding bank holidays — for non-urgent appointment requests, medication queries and admin requests, with safeguards against urgent clinical requests being erroneously submitted online. No routine "switching off" except by agreement with the commissioner. Practices cannot cap online submission numbers (parity with phone/walk-in is required). A practice email mailbox does not satisfy this requirement. Underlying regs: GMS Reg. 71ZD / PMS Reg. 64ZD.
   Source: [Changes to the GP Contract in 2025/26](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/changes-to-the-gp-contract-in-2025-26/); [Online consultations FAQs](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/online-consultations-frequently-asked-questions/); [BMA guidance PDF](https://www.bma.org.uk/media/xhoihr5f/bma-faqs-for-1-october-2025-online-consultations-v2.pdf)

2. **"You and Your General Practice" (YYGP) patient charter** — every practice must **link to the NHS England YYGP document from its website**, no later than 1 October 2025, and reflect its standards in how the practice communicates. Practices must also be prepared to receive and respond to patient feedback referencing YYGP (channels: in person, phone, online via the practice website, or NHS App, during 8am–6.30pm core hours).
   Source: [You and Your General Practice](https://www.england.nhs.uk/publication/you-and-your-general-practice/); [Changes to the GP Contract in 2025/26](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/changes-to-the-gp-contract-in-2025-26/)

3. **GP Connect: Access Record + Update Record enabled** by 1 October 2025 — read-only record access for other NHS/private direct-care providers with patient permission, and community pharmacy consultation summaries flowing into practice workflow. This is a backend clinical-systems requirement, not website content, but it is assessed as part of the same October compliance package.
   Source: [Assessment of general practice compliance — 1 October 2025 contractual changes](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/assessment-of-general-practice-compliance-1-october-contractual-changes/)

4. ICBs can issue **remedial breach notices** for non-compliance with these three items.

## C. Accessibility — legal minimum, and a discrepancy worth flagging

- PRN00651 (as last edited Sept 2025) still states the legal floor as **"WCAG 2.1 AA"** verbatim: *"All existing websites must be fully compliant to the standard WCAG 2.1 AA no later than the 23 September 2020"* and *"GP practice websites must: Meet at least level AA of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG 2.1) and aim for AAA where possible."* — [PRN00651 PDF p.15, p.69](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/PRN00651-creating-a-highly-usable-and-accessible-gp-website-for-patients-v3.pdf)
- However, the underlying **Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) Accessibility Regulations 2018** contain a self-updating clause: as WCAG is revised, the regulatory conformance target updates automatically. GOV.UK's own guidance (current as of 2025/2026) states the target is now **WCAG 2.2 AA**. Source: [GOV.UK accessibility requirements](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/accessibility-requirements-for-public-sector-websites-and-apps)
- **Practical implication for the demo site**: WCAG 2.2 AA should be the design target (the brief already states this) — NHS England's own GP-specific guidance is simply stale on the exact version number, a known hazard of a document last substantively written in 2022–2023. Cite both: state you're meeting the *current* legal floor (2.2 AA) which *exceeds* what the GP-specific guide names.
- Required accessibility-statement content (plain English): compliance status (fully/partially/not compliant), which parts aren't compliant and why (exemption or disproportionate burden), how patients can get inaccessible content another way, and how to report accessibility problems / escalate. Sample: GOV.UK's [sample accessibility statement](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/model-accessibility-statement). Source: [PRN00651 PDF p.16](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/PRN00651-creating-a-highly-usable-and-accessible-gp-website-for-patients-v3.pdf)
- **Named accessibility anti-patterns** (explicitly called out as failure points, in order of frequency): (1) outdated/unmaintained templates, (2) written content that fails the NHS content style guide / reading-age target, (3) missing alt text, (4) PDFs used instead of HTML. Also explicitly banned: **accessibility overlay widgets/toolbars** (NHS Digital service manual and Scope guidance both say these don't create genuine accessibility — they're a red flag, not a fix), and **pop-up/overlay banners generally** (see D.6 below).
- Accessible Information Standard (DAPB1605, legal since 1 Aug 2016): if a GP would normally direct a patient to information on the practice website, staff must check the patient can actually use the website for that purpose, and provide an alternative format if not. Source: [Accessible Information Standard](https://www.england.nhs.uk/accessible-information-standard/)

## D. Structure, navigation and usability rules (all sourced to PRN00651 Part 3, unless noted)

1. **Primary navigation: 5–7 items maximum, no dropdown sub-menus.** Recommended 6-item menu: **Home · Appointments · Prescriptions · Services · About the surgery · Contact us.** "News" should *not* be in the main menu (patients rarely browse it; put time-bound items on the relevant task page instead, with an auto-expiry date). [PRN00651 p.35](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/PRN00651-creating-a-highly-usable-and-accessible-gp-website-for-patients-v3.pdf)
2. **Search box must be visibly labelled "Search"**, not just a magnifying-glass icon — icon-only search was measured to reduce use, especially on mobile. [p.38]
3. **Mobile nav**: no bare hamburger icon; use the word "Menu"/"More", or NHS.uk's "combo nav" pattern (first two items + "More"). [p.38]
4. **Homepage**: quick links to the top tasks, no paragraphs of prose, phone number + opening times visible (header/footer OK). 80% of users start on the homepage; the more unrelated options on the page, the more users get lost. [pp.38–39]
5. **Breadcrumbs** on any page reached via a linked-out journey (e.g. PCN pages); never auto-open new tabs; always give a "Home" link. [p.54]
6. **Recommended top-task call-to-action wording** (tested with patients): "Make, change or cancel an appointment" / "Get a repeat prescription online" / "Get a sick note for work" / "Get test results" / "Join the surgery" (patients were split between "join" and "register" — "register" got confused with "Register for Patient Access"). [p.34]
7. **No pop-ups, overlays, or interstitials of any kind** — 27% of patients who saw an overlay first struggled to get past it (worse on mobile); once dismissed, the info is gone from the site entirely. Time-bound/urgent information should live embedded in the relevant page (e.g. a closure notice on Contact Us / Appointments) using the **NHS Design System warning callout pattern**, used sparingly, with a hard expiry date. [pp.17, 45–47] — **This is one of the single most emphasised "don't" in the whole guidance** and is worth checking directly against the demo site's home-page alert banner design.
8. **Fax numbers should be removed entirely**; **email should not appear on/near clinical-request pages** (Contact Us, Appointments) — reserve email for narrow cases like formal complaints; any non-online-consultation "contact form" needs clinical-safety-officer sign-off before use for clinical requests. [p.39]
9. **PCN-only content should not require patients to visit a separate PCN website** unless unavoidable — if it does, it must be explicitly signposted and linked directly to the relevant page, not just to the PCN homepage. [p.53]

## E. Content/writing-style rules (source: PRN00651 §1.2/§3.2, plus [NHS Digital service manual content style guide](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/content))

- **Reading age 9–11 (US Grade 4–5)**, tested with the **Hemingway Editor** — if genuinely unachievable for complex medical content, fall back to an 11–14-year reading age, not higher.
- Plain English rules given as explicit numbers: **sentences ≤ 20 words, paragraphs ≤ 3 sentences**, active voice ("find a pharmacy" not "a pharmacy can be found"), short words ("have"/"get" not "experience").
- Links must **start with a verb** ("Request an appointment", "Register with the surgery"), must make sense out of context, must say if they leave the site — never "click here".
- No jargon; check terms against the **A to Z of NHS health writing**.
- **Specific terminology NHS England tested with patients and now mandates/recommends**:
  - Do **not** use "online consultation" anywhere on the public-facing site — 83% of patients tested didn't know or were unsure what it meant. Use task-based phrasing instead: "request an appointment online", "get a sick note", "request test results".
  - Do **not** use "emergency appointment" — confuses patients with A&E. Use a timeframe instead ("Urgent appointments: today").
  - Use "**consultation**" as the umbrella word for any doctor/nurse contact, whatever the channel (phone, face-to-face, video, text/email).
  - Use "**by text or email**", not "secure message".
  - Use "**doctor, nurse or other healthcare professional**", not "clinician(s)".
  - Do **not** use the word "**triage**" in patient-facing copy — use "we will look at the information you give us and decide the most suitable person for you to see and when they are available".
- **PDFs are explicitly discouraged/should be avoided** — content should be HTML, not PDF, both for accessibility and because translated/PDF content is not screen-reader or translation-tool friendly.
- Images: only where there's a genuine user need (no decorative images); never put text inside an image (unreadable to screen readers and untranslatable); every image needs real alt text describing information/function, not "image of...".
- **Official downloadable content templates exist** (PRN00651 §5, pp.72–83) for: Appointments, Prescriptions, Contact us, Test results, Sick (fit) notes, Changing your contact details, Register with the surgery, Accessing someone else's information (proxy access), Managing your health online — pre-tested with low-literacy/low-digital-confidence patients; guidance is to adapt them minimally rather than write from scratch.

## F. Page-by-page content checklist (mapping directly onto the demo site's IA)

**Home** — quick links/CTAs to the 8 top tasks (ranked, per patient research): 1) make/change/cancel appointment, 2) repeat prescription, 3) sick note, 4) test results, 5) register with practice, 6) phone number, 7) opening times, 8) address. No prose paragraphs. [PRN00651 p.6, p.39]

**Appointments** (rated the single most important/critical page): clear separated sections for urgent vs routine, using time-based labels ("today"/"this week") not "emergency/urgent/routine"; phone number, opening times, and the online-request link all in one place; explicit response-time expectation; avoid "online consultation" wording; home-visit criteria and how to request one. [pp.42–44, 74–75]

**Prescriptions** (2nd most important page): ordering routes (NHS App / practice system / paper), nominating/changing a pharmacy, medication queries route, medication reviews, prescription charges, disposal of old medicines, "about pharmacists" info. [pp.44, 76–77]

**Contact us** (3rd critical page — should mirror the practice's nhs.uk profile): phone number, address + directions/transport, opening times (including holiday variations), out-of-hours routing (NHS 111 online / 111 / 999), building accessibility features, parking. [pp.45, 78–79]

**Services** menu should link to: fit/sick notes, test results, vaccinations, **self-referral** (this is explicitly named as Services-menu content in the official guidance — directly validates the demo site's Pharmacy First / first-contact physio / Talking Therapies / sexual health / stop smoking pages), registering with the surgery, proxy access ("accessing someone else's information"), clinics, healthy-living/local support, submitting readings, "managing your health online", accessing your health record. [p.37]

**About the surgery / Practice information** should link to: surgery staff, policies and procedures, complaints and feedback, news/blog, **CQC rating**, **GP earnings**, accessibility statement. [p.37] The demo site's existing set (feedback/complaints, records/GDPR privacy, registration without ID, carers, chaperones, zero tolerance, PPG) matches the *statutory* content list in section A almost item-for-item — the two gaps against the official checklist are **CQC rating** and **GP net earnings**, both of which are explicit contractual publication requirements, not just good practice.

**"Managing your health online"** — a distinct recommended page (with its own official template) explaining, tool by tool, what patients can do in the online consultation tool vs the NHS App vs a practice-system app (Patient Access/SystmOnline) — this exists precisely because patient research found switching between different branded tools was a major source of confusion. Willowbrook's current IA doesn't appear to have this page; consider adding it or folding it into Appointments/Prescriptions with very explicit "use X for Y" framing. [pp.40, 82–83]

## G. GP website benchmarking and improvement tool — what it actually measures

Primary source: [publication page](https://www.england.nhs.uk/publication/gp-website-benchmarking-and-improvement-tool/) and [xlsx](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/PRN00274-gp-website-benchmarking-and-improvement-tool.xlsx) (PRN00274, published 30 May 2023); corroborated by PRN00651 §1.6 (pp.13–14, which reproduces a sample screenshot of the tool).

- **50 criteria**, scored **Well / Adequate / Inadequate**, organised in **3 sections**: (1) patient-priority items mapped to the 7 core patient journeys (appointments, prescriptions, sick notes, test results, registration, phone/opening-times/address findability), (2) practice-manager/content-owner priorities (contractually-required info, FFT, registration, boundary), (3) legal/compliance items (privacy notices, accessibility statement, cookie policy).
- Explicitly distinguishes **supplier-owned issues** (template structure, navigation, mobile responsiveness, technical accessibility) from **practice-owned issues** (content accuracy, reading level, freshness) — useful framing for a studio selling a template, since it tells you which parts of your product need to be right out-of-the-box vs which parts are the client's ongoing responsibility.
- Sample row shown in PRN00651 (Item 1, "Overlays and pop-ups"): scored "Well" = **zero** overlays anywhere on the site (cookie banners excluded); "Adequate" = only a dismissible cookie pop-up, nothing else; "Inadequate" = any other pop-up present anywhere on the site. This is a hard, binary-leaning criterion, not a soft preference.
- Estimated audit time: **45–90 minutes** per website. All practices in England are expected to self-audit using this tool; ICBs are encouraged to run it too and fund remediation. [PRN00651 p.13]

## H. Ongoing-maintenance cadence (from PRN01429, "Step-by-step guide," 3 Feb 2025 — quoted directly)

- "Reviewing key pages every 6 months, all other content annually. This could be done on a rolling basis, with a certain percentage of content checked each month."
- "Using the NHS England 'GP website benchmark and improve tool' to check the website every 12–18 months."
- "Considering whether to update to a latest GP website template every couple of years."
- The guide's own four-stage lifecycle: **Stage 1 Plan → Stage 2 Improve content → Stage 3 Migrate template → Stage 4 Maintain**, each with numbered tasks (e.g. "Task 1.1: Leadership and initial goal setting").
Source: [Step-by-step guide to improving general practice website online journeys](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/step-by-step-guide-to-improving-general-practice-website-online-journeys/), PRN01429.

## Gap check against the current Willowbrook Surgery demo site

Given the site's current pages (Home, Appointments, Prescriptions, Services & clinics, Our team, Practice information, Contact, Accessibility statement), here is where it already matches official guidance well, and where it's short of the checklist above:

**Already aligned**
- 6-ish-item nav roughly matching the recommended IA (Home/Appointments/Prescriptions/Services/Practice info/Contact — very close to the official 6-item recommendation).
- Practice information page bundles feedback/complaints, GDPR/records privacy, registration-without-ID, carers, chaperones, zero tolerance, PPG — this maps almost 1:1 onto the "About the surgery" statutory content list in section A/F above.
- Self-referral routes (Pharmacy First, first-contact physio, NHS Talking Therapies, sexual health, stop smoking) under Services & clinics directly matches the officially-named "self-referral" content item for the Services section (§F).
- Separate Accessibility statement page exists (legally required regardless of demo status).

**Gaps against the checklist above (worth deciding on deliberately, even for a sales-demo site, since a studio pitching NHS-conventions credibility will be judged on exactly this list)**
1. **No visible CQC rating or GP net-earnings disclosure** on Practice information — both are explicit statutory publication items (section A) and are the two items patients familiar with real practice websites will notice missing.
2. **Home-page alert banner** — confirm it is an *inline, embedded* NHS Design System-style warning callout on the page itself (compliant) rather than anything overlay/dismiss-based (the guidance's most emphatic "don't", with a measured 27% failure rate on mobile). Given the brief calls it a "dismissable-less alert banner," it sounds compliant with the "no overlay, embed in-page, no easy-dismiss-and-lose-it" pattern — worth stating that explicitly as the design rationale, citing this guidance, since it's a good differentiator vs the many real practice sites still using overlays.
3. **"Online consultation" terminology** — check nothing on the Appointments/Home pages uses that phrase; NHS England's own tested finding (83% of patients didn't understand it) is a strong, citable reason to use "request an appointment online" instead throughout.
4. **No "Managing your health online" page** distinguishing the online-consultation tool vs NHS App vs any practice-system app — worth adding given this was identified as a specific, tested source of patient confusion, and it's one of the ten official downloadable templates.
5. **Practice boundary map / postcode checker** — not mentioned in the current page list; this is a statutory practice-leaflet item (section A) and commonly expected on a "Registration" or "Contact" page.
6. **YYGP charter link** — for authenticity as a 2025/26-accurate demo, a Practice information/Home footer link to "You and Your General Practice" would be the single most currently-topical addition (contractual as of 1 Oct 2025), and costs little to add as a static link.
7. **Online consultation core-hours messaging** (8am–6.30pm Mon–Fri) — if the demo depicts an online request form, showing the core-hours availability statement and the "we'll respond by end of next working day if submitted outside these hours" pattern from the official Appointments template (section F/PRN00651 §5.2) would be a concrete, currently-accurate detail to include.
8. Confirm **WCAG 2.2 AA** is the stated target in the accessibility statement copy (not "2.1 AA") — see the version discrepancy flagged in section C; 2.2 AA is the actual current legal floor even though NHS England's own GP-specific PDF still says 2.1.

## Hazards / stale-information flags

- **PRN00651's "Part 4" contractual table is self-flagged as non-definitive** ("a guide to requirements at the time of writing (July 2022) not a definitive list… check if requirements have changed") — treat its regulation citations as a strong starting point but not a substitute for checking the current GMS/PMS Regulations directly if this checklist is ever used for a real (non-demo) client engagement.
- **WCAG version**: NHS England's GP-specific guidance still names WCAG 2.1 AA; the actual current statutory floor (via the Public Sector Bodies Accessibility Regs 2018's self-updating clause) is **WCAG 2.2 AA** per GOV.UK. Use 2.2 AA as the real target; cite GOV.UK, not the NHS PDF, for the version number.
- **Online consultation core-hours mandate (8am–6.30pm) is very recent (1 Oct 2025)** — it is not mentioned anywhere in PRN00651 (2022/2023) or the benchmarking tool (2023); it only appears in the 2025/26 contract-change communications. Any checklist or sales pitch claiming "NHS-compliant" needs both generations of source cited, since older secondary commentary (blog posts, agency guides) written before October 2025 will be missing this entirely.
- I could not access the literal cell-by-cell contents of the 50-item benchmarking xlsx (binary spreadsheet); the three-section structure and the one worked example (overlays) are directly sourced from the official PDF and publication page, but the other 49 rows are not individually reproduced here.
